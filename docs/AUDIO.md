# Audio

The music (five tracks: the menu, a climb theme for each stage of the tower, and the
lament), what moves over a run and on whose terms -- the theme, the key, the tempo -- the
cut into the lament when he falls, the sound effects and how they were mixed with nobody
listening, the sample path, and the offline previews and tests that stand in for ears.
Read this when touching `src/render/audio.js`, `src/render/sfx.js`,
`src/render/gamesounds.js`, `src/render/tracks.js`, `tools/compose-music.mjs`, any tool
that renders or tests sound, or anything that makes a sound.
See also: [BUILD.md](BUILD.md) (muting: `M`, `--mute`, `?mute` and the sticky dev flag),
[TESTING.md](TESTING.md) (the suites in the fast run), [TOWER.md](TOWER.md) (the zones the
music follows), STATUS.md (nobody has listened to any of it),
[../DELEGATION.md](../DELEGATION.md) (why the music is generated by a script and not
prompted for), and the `game-audio-mixing` skill.

## What plays

Five tracks in `src/render/tracks.js`, every one composed by `tools/compose-music.mjs`
from scale steps and chord charts. None of the local model's notes are left: the lament
was the last of them, and it was recomposed (below).

| track | what it is | key, written tempo | length |
|---|---|---|---|
| `menu` | a brass fanfare on THE CALL that opens into a song in thirds and sixths | E major (the fanfare E Mixolydian), 176 bpm | 20 bars |
| `below` | stage one, BELOW: a sneaking march, staccato root-and-fifth bass | E minor, 144 bpm | 32 bars |
| `above` | stage two, THE WORLD ABOVE: a galloping bass under a sawtooth lead | D minor, 168 bpm | 32 bars |
| `heavens` | stage three, THE HEAVENS: a soft triangle lead over a plucked harp | E Lydian, 150 bpm | 32 bars |
| `gameover` | the lament: a gothic organ and a music box -- a fall, a stab at the impact, a loop of suspensions | E minor, 72 bpm | two bars that play once, eight that loop |

Every track has a lead, a bass and a choir; the menu and the lament also have `brass`,
block chords (sawtooth in the menu; in the lament, the organ's manual). A note is a name,
`null` for a rest, or an array of names for a chord (`pitchesOf` in `audio.js`: one
oscillator per name on one envelope) -- chords and the fourth voice arrived with the menu's
brass and are inert for every other track. A track's `mix` sets a voice's oscillator, gain,
envelope (`gate`, `pad`, `pluck`, or the lament's `organ`) or `stops` (the ranks each
pitch sounds on) over `VOICE_MIX`, and every track sets some of its own, by measurement. A
track also carries its `tonic`; the lament carries `intro: 2` (bars that play once),
`landing: 1` (the bar the engine jumps to at the impact) and `keyFromCut: true` (below).

**THE CALL** -- the tonic, up a fifth, the step below and back, the octave -- is the motif
the three climb themes share, so they are one game's music as it climbs. The menu's
fanfare plays it, the milestone fanfares play it, a companion joining plays its head, and
the lament's music box opens every loop with it turned upside down. test-music finds it at
least twice a loop in every climb theme and in the menu's brass.

The climb themes are 32 bars, 46 to 53 seconds at their written tempo, because the first
stage alone lasts 900 floors and the old 16-bar climb came round every 24 seconds. They
are composed by a script rather than prompted for because a riff over a bass over
sustained chords has exact arithmetic in it, and the local model could not hold it (see
[../DELEGATION.md](../DELEGATION.md), round three): `compose-music.mjs` asserts every bar
of every voice is exactly sixteen sixteenths as it builds it, so a voice that does not
add up stops the build instead of drifting against the others twenty minutes into a run.
`tracks.js` is GENERATED; see [Regenerating the music](#regenerating-the-music).

## How the engine plays it

- **A lookahead scheduler**, not a timer per note: `pump()` runs every 25 ms and queues
  notes about 100 ms ahead against the AudioContext's own clock. Browser timers drift
  while the main thread is drawing; the audio clock does not.
- **One song clock per theme.** Every note is timed from its place in the music,
  sixteenths from the top, on a clock all the theme's voices read (`advance`, `startSet`).
  Each voice used to keep its own, a running sum of its note lengths at the tempo when
  each was queued, and while the tempo moved they walked apart and never came back: ten
  minutes of the heavens put its lead 0.96 s off its harp.
- **Each voice wraps at its own length**, to its first note, or for a track with an
  `intro` to the first note after it.
- **Each theme has its own pair of buses**, one for the lead, bass and brass and one into
  the choir's 2.4 kHz low-pass, so a handover can fade one theme while the next swells
  without touching the shared music level. The whole music bus goes through a 5.2 kHz
  low-pass, and everything leaves through a limiter at -6 dBFS.
- **Handovers run on the audio clock** (`crossTo`): the cut is a time and a place in the
  music, and `pump()` stops the old theme's notes at it and queues the new theme's from
  it. The old `crossTo` faded out and then set a `setTimeout` to start the next track,
  which a death, a pause or a second handover in that window left to fire afterwards.

## What changes over a run, and on whose terms

Three things change while he climbs: the THEME, one per stage; the KEY, a step with each
zone of a stage; and the TEMPO, which leans with how hard the climb is going. The user
found that the music changed too much, and at random, and measured over real runs it
did: a companion joining or slipping moved the whole piece into another key
the moment it happened, mid-phrase; the key stepped every 200 floors, out of step with
the zones, onto whatever note came next; the tempo swung up to 35% in one bar of a
human-paced climb; and past the first lap a theme could be handed over five bars after it
began. Each of them now changes slowly and only on the music's own terms, and
`tools/test-steady.mjs` holds all of it over real runs.

Everything the climb's music does happens when a ZONE arrives (`Audio.followClimb`), on
the music's next bar line, together with the zone's chime. `render/gamesounds.js` feeds
it the highest floor reached -- `Math.max(run.maxFloor, player.floor)` -- never the
current one, or standing on a zone's first floor and hopping down and back would change
the music twice a second. And it feeds it BEFORE the step's own sounds are built: the game
raises `maxFloor` only after `handleEvents` returns, so the key used to follow a step
late, and every sound of the landing that reached a new zone, its chime included, was
built in the old key and rang a whole tone under the theme it was ringing over.

### The theme: one per stage, handed over on a bar line

`src/game/stages.js` says which theme each zone plays, by the zone's NAME (past floor 2300
every cycle of zones is a reshuffle, so an index would put the cellar march in the
heavens): BELOW is BASEMENT, DUNGEON, FOREST, SWAMP and DOWNTOWN; THE WORLD ABOVE is
CITADEL, STORM and ABYSS; THE HEAVENS is NEBULA, COSMOS, STARFIELD and ZENITH.

A zone of another stage hands the music over. The playing theme fades to
`HANDOVER_FROM` (0.3) of full level over at least `HANDOVER_FADE` (1.5 s) and is cut on
its own next bar line; the new theme's first downbeat lands on that cut at the same level,
in its home key, and swells to full over `HANDOVER_SWELL` (1.2 s) while the old one's last
tails let go over `HANDOVER_TAIL` (0.08 s). That is 1.5 to 3.2 s at 144 bpm, around the
2.2 s the zone's title stays up. The outgoing theme plays on in the key and at the tempo it
had when the handover began: its cut is a bar line worked out at that tempo, and following
the live tempo brought it to the line early. It first faded all the way to silence at the
cut, and a linear ramp to zero spends its last tenth under -20 dB: rendered, 0.5 s of the
music more than 12 dB down and 0.2 s at -57 to -65 dB, a gap before every new theme.

Not before the playing theme has played `THEME_MIN_LOOPS` (one) whole loop, and
`KEY_MIN_BARS` since its last change of key. A zone of another stage that comes sooner
waits, and the handover is cut on the bar line where the wait ends -- the loop's end, the
one seam every theme already has -- unless a zone of the playing theme's stage arrives in
the meantime and calls it off. It does not wait for the next zone instead, because a human
takes one to three minutes over a zone and the wrong stage's theme would stay on that long.
In the first lap nothing waits: the stages last 900, 600 and 800 floors, many loops, and
every handover is where it always was. Past it every lap is a fresh shuffle, and the themes
used to hand over at nearly every zone -- the attract bot's second lap handed ABOVE back to
BELOW five bars after it had begun.

`startClimb(floor)` puts the right stage's theme on at once, on the rung of the key its
zone has reached: a new run at 0, or one picked up mid-climb when sound first becomes
available.

### The key: a step with each zone, on its bar line

`KEY_BANDS` in `audio.js` is the ladder, `[0, 2, 3, 5, 7, 5, 3, 2]` semitones over the
playing theme's home key: degrees of the natural minor climbing to a fifth and easing back
down. Each zone of the playing theme's stage takes the next rung, and the ladder restarts
with every theme, so each opens in its own home key. In the first lap the rungs are the
zones' places in their stage: BELOW at 0, 2, 3, 5, 7, THE WORLD ABOVE at 0, 2, 3, THE
HEAVENS at 0, 2, 3, 5. A fifth is the ceiling, and test-music checks every voice of every
theme stays playable up to it.

- **On a bar line.** The change goes on the first bar line no voice has been queued past
  (`freeBar`), and every note from that place in the music plays in the new key, however far
  ahead each voice has been queued (`keyNext`). Applied at once, as it was, a key change
  landed on whatever note the scheduler queued next -- a sixteenth into the lead, a bar into
  the choir.
- **With its chime.** The zone's chime is queued on that same downbeat (`sfxTheme`, from
  `arrival`), in the key from there, and the tempo is held still until the bar line so it
  comes exactly when the chime was told it would. The bar line is at most a bar and a
  lookahead away, 1.9 s at the slowest tempo.
- **Never twice in `KEY_MIN_BARS`** (8 bars), counting a theme's first downbeat as a change:
  a zone that arrives sooner keeps the key it arrived in (its chime still rings).
- **One live key.** `setKey` sets it at once, for a run starting at a floor or a test;
  `resetKeys` puts it back to the home key with nothing left waiting (a new run, the
  impact, the menu); `transpose` is what the scheduler and every effect read. It was two
  layers that added: the ladder, and a companion's lift, -5 to +7 semitones, set the moment
  one joined and cleared when they slipped. And the ladder stepped every 200 floors from the
  stage's entry, where BELOW's zones change at 100, 300, 500 and 700, so the zone and the key
  changed alternately about every hundred floors. **Companions no longer touch the key.**
  Their call still plays; `COMPANION_KEY` is only the register of their babble now.
- **Held for the lament** (`pinned`): see [The lament](#the-lament).

### The tempo: leaning with the run, slowly

A climb theme's tempo follows `game.intensity`, which is his momentum and the length of
the combo, toward `tempoTargetFor(i)`: between `TEMPO_MIN` x0.92 and `TEMPO_MAX` x1.30 of
the theme's written bpm, on a curve that stays low until the run is really going. It eases
at `TEMPO_EASE` (0.15 of the gap a second, a time constant of about 7 s) and never moves
more than `TEMPO_BAR_MAX` (2%) in one bar as heard (`easeTempo`). The range was x0.92 to
x1.70 eased at 2.8 a second: a human-paced climb swung up to 35% in a single bar and moved
more than 3% in four bars of five, and the bot sat pinned at x1.70, 245 bpm for a march
written at 144. The song lurched; it did not lift. Notes already queued keep their timing.

It is held still while a zone's bar line is on its way (above), and **while paused**:
`setIntensity` does nothing while the engine is paused, the choir with it. `main.js` goes on
calling it every frame on the pause screen with an intensity of 0, and at the climb's slow
pace a 20 s pause brought the march back 15% slower in one bar (x1.13 to x0.97), then crept
up for half a minute: the lurch the easing is there to prevent, made by the easing.

**Off the climb** -- the menu and the lament -- nothing drives the tempo, so nothing is
smoothed: they start at their written tempo and settle to `TEMPO_MIN` at `TEMPO_SETTLE`
(2.8 a second), within a bar (`tempoStep`). The climb's slow easing was first applied to
them too, and the title music sagged 8% over its first 25 s. `main.js` drives intensity 0
on every screen but a climb, and nothing at all while he falls, so the lament's fall plays
at its written 72 bpm and the scoreboard's loop at about 66.

The choir bus swells with the run between `CHOIR_MIN` (0.06) and `CHOIR_MAX` (0.30),
eased at `CHOIR_EASE`, so at full tilt the choir carries the track and the riff rides it.

## The riff does not vary, and that was tried

The climb track briefly carried POOLS of alternate arrangements -- four leads, three
basses, three choirs -- that the engine swapped between at every loop point. It was
asked for, it was built, it was heard, and it was removed: **a riff that quietly becomes
a different riff every twenty-four seconds is not variety, it is the track losing its
nerve.** What changes over a run is the theme, the key and the tempo, each slowly and on
the music's terms. A new theme at a new stage is a different thing: it arrives with the
zone's title, and it is the climb being somewhere else. Do not rebuild the pools. The
commit is in the history if the argument ever needs having again.

## The menu

Asked for: a theme that starts fast and loud, big brassy chords opening into a lovely
harmony. The menu before it was the climb's hook in E Dorian at 138
bpm, one square note at a time, the slowest thing on the soundtrack after the lament.

Then, of the build that had it (2026-09-28): the user wanted the melody to arrive sooner,
and found the loud opening a little overdone. The fanfare was
eight bars, the call four times -- twelve seconds of brass before the song, 1.8 LU over it,
every stab a five-note chord. It is half that now.

E, 176 bpm written -- about 162 as heard, because off the climb the tempo rests at
`TEMPO_MIN` -- and 20 bars, 27.3 s written and 29.6 s as heard:

- **bars 1-4, the fanfare, E Mixolydian.** The brass plays THE CALL twice, on E and then on
  B, as sawtooth block chords of four notes (five on each call's first chord), each call
  answered by major chords climbing a step at a time; the second answer lands on the
  dominant, so the song arrives. Bar 1 beat 1 is a five-note E chord with the bass and the
  choir: no intro. The song starts about six seconds in.
- **bars 5-18, the song, E major.** The brass stops and the lead sings two notes at a time,
  the melody with a third or a sixth below it, over three-note choir chords and a rolling
  bass. It opens on the call again, twice as slow, on the fifth.
- **bars 19-20, the way back.** The brass returns on C and D and drives eighths into bar
  1's E, so the loop comes round on the fanfare instead of settling into its quiet half.

It is now the fastest thing written; the lament is still the slowest. Its levels were set
at the level the title screen plays it (intensity 0, the choir bus at `CHOIR_MIN`):
-14.8 LUFS over the loop against the climb themes' -13.8 to -14.9 at the previews' gain,
the fanfare -14.1, the song -15.2 and the way back -14.0. The first levels had it the wrong
way round, the song 1.4 LU over the fanfare; the eight-bar fanfare then had it 1.8 over,
which was the overdone part. `tools/music-render.mjs` reads the sections it measures from
the track (`menuSections`), so a fanfare of another length is measured as itself.

## The lament

What plays from the moment the fire takes him until he climbs again.

It has been rewritten twice on complaints. The first was the local model's three bars,
played in whatever key the climb had reached and dropped to E at the impact, mid-phrase.
The second (2026-09-23) was THE CALL turned upside down in the soundtrack's own idiom --
square lead, triangle bass, choir, a little brass -- and the user found it poor and asked
for something in the manner of The Phantom of the Opera, but 8-bit, a spooky death held in
suspense (2026-09-28). That musical is under copyright and nothing of it is used; what was asked
for is its STYLE, the gothic pipe organ, and the one quotation is where that idiom comes
from and is free: the opening gesture of Bach's Toccata and Fugue in D minor, BWV 565.

**The piece** is a gothic organ and a music box in E minor at 72 bpm (about 66 on the
scoreboard): two bars that play once (`intro: 2`) and eight that loop.

- **The fall**, bar 1, cut short by the landing wherever it has got to -- 0.4 s in from
  the ground floor, at most 2.3 s in from high up, so its tightening is written into its
  first eleven sixteenths. The organ pedal throbbing as a heartbeat whose beats come closer
  (four sixteenths apart, then three, three, two, two) and creep up by semitones, E F F# G
  G# A; a low cluster in the choir growing a note at a time, E, F, the tritone A#, B (all
  four by 1.7 s in), each a semitone from a note of the stab's chord, which it moves into;
  the music box falling chromatically from B high above. The organ's manual is silent: its
  first chord is the stab. As heard (test-lament, THE FALL AS HEARD) the second half is 1.2
  to 1.4 LU over the first and no 100 ms is more than 5 dB under the fall's level. The
  first version of this fall had its cluster on the pad envelope, which fades each chord
  from three quarters of its length while the next swells in from silence; it fell in a
  heartbeat's rest, so half a second before every high fall's impact the music dropped 12-15
  dB for 0.3 s and came back 19 LU up 0.3 s before the stab -- a false hit -- and the second
  half measured 1 LU under the first. Its notes passed every check; only listening to the
  render found it.
- **The stab**, bar 2, the impact: a diminished seventh, D# F# A C, over the tonic E in
  the pedal -- the Toccata's diminished chord over its tonic pedal -- with the choir
  holding it and the music box striking it; then the organ alone plays the Toccata's
  gesture moved to E minor, the mordent B A B held and the plunge A G F# E to the leading
  tone, over the dominant in the pedal (the diminished chord over B is its minor ninth),
  into the loop's tonic.
- **The loop**, bars 3-10, the scoreboard, 29.0 s as heard. The pedal sinks a semitone a
  bar, E D# D C# C B, the Baroque lament bass, then the Neapolitan's A and the dominant's
  B. The organ holds the E minor triad as the bass moves under it (Em, Em over its major
  seventh, C# half-diminished -- Em6 -- C major seventh, Em over B), through diminished
  sevenths on D#, C# and F#, and lets notes hang on the half bar: 7-6 over D and over C#,
  4-3 over B twice (the second unprepared), and 9-8 over E ACROSS THE SEAM, so even home
  hangs for two beats. The pedal is on the tonic only in the loop's first bar: a half
  cadence on B in bar 6, the Neapolitan F over A, the dominant, and home only round the
  seam. The music box plays a slow broken-chord lullaby over it all, opening every loop
  with THE CALL turned over (the thread from the climb's music), and from bar 7 of 10 the
  choir doubles the organ, a rank drawn for the second half.

**The organ is a mix, and a small engine extension** (tracks' `mix` in
`compose-music.mjs`, `stopsOf` and the `organ` envelope in `audio.js`). The brass voice is
the manual: squares on the `organ` envelope, which speaks over `ORGAN_SPEAK` (30 ms,
where a gate's attack is 6 ms), holds with no decay and rings `ORGAN_RING` (80 ms) past
its length, so chords join legato; and two STOPS, the written pitch and a rank an octave
up 7 cents sharp at half level, so every pitch is two pulse channels in octaves beating
slowly, which is how an 8-bit score gets a pipe organ's chorus. The bass is the pedal,
triangle with its octave rank; the choir, a triangle on the same `organ` envelope through
its low-pass, is the soft flue (on the `pad` envelope it swelled and faded in every chord,
which is not a pipe and opened a hole in the fall); the lead is the music box, a triangle struck and left to ring (`pluck`). A
voice's `stops` is `[{ semi, cents, gain }]`, one oscillator per stop per pitch on the
note's envelope; with none, a pitch is one oscillator as before. `test-music` counts the
ranks in every voice's range and holds a stop to two octaves, under a quarter tone of
detune and no louder than its voice.

**The landing** (`Audio.landLament`). The fall is 1.0 s from the ground floor to 2.45 s
from floor 654 up, and the lament comes in 0.15 to 0.6 s after the catch; the second
lament's landing chord was written into its fall at a fixed 1.7 s, so it came 0.2 to 1.2 s
off the splat, early from high up and late from low down. Now the track names its stab bar
(`landing: 1`) and `gamesounds.js`'s `onImpact` asks the engine to jump there: the stab is
struck `LANDING_LEAD` (0.03 s) after the ask, with the splat, which plays at once --
measured 31 to 44 ms after the impact over every stage and fall length (the rest is the
frame the impact is seen on). A beat was the obvious grid and is 0.83 s at 72 bpm; the fall
has no downbeat to keep and the stab is where the pulse starts again, so the jump is made at
any instant. The engine queues every voice to the lookahead, moves the song clock on so the
stab's place in the score falls at the landing (every position after keeps its place in the
score), stops the fall's notes queued to start at or after it before they start, lets the
fall's buses go under it over `LANDING_CUT` (0.02 s) and plays on a fresh pair, carrying on
the lament's swell in if it is still swelling. Every voice has a note where the stab starts
(`compose-music.mjs` refuses one that does not). A skipped fall never lands, so the
scoreboard's arrival lands it (it does nothing after a real landing); and `main.js` lands a
lament it starts late -- sound first coming up on the scoreboard, or music switched back on
in the options opened from it -- so it opens on the stab, not in a fall that is over. Only
once he has landed (`game.impacted`, or the options opened from the scoreboard): O opens
the options from anywhere, the fall included, and at first any setting changed there struck
the stab with him frozen in mid-air. The
stab is 4 to 9 LU over the fall just before it, peaking within the climb's own peak.

**The cut.** `game.onFallStart` (`render/gamesounds.js`) calls `audio.lament()`, with no
`stopMusic()` and no `resetKeys()` ahead of it. The climb sags to `LAMENT_FROM` (0.4) over
at least `LAMENT_FADE` (0.15 s) and is cut on its own next BEAT (`LAMENT_GRID`, 4
sixteenths), where the lament's fall comes in at that level and rises to full over
`LAMENT_SWELL` (0.25 s). A beat, not the stage handover's bar line: a bar of the march is
1.7 s and the whole fall can be 1.0 s, while a beat is at most 0.42 s. The lament starts at
its written tempo, and the choir bus is taken to `CHOIR_MIN`, where the scoreboard keeps
it. If he falls while a stage handover is waiting for its bar line, the old theme, still
the one heard, is cut on its own next beat and the cued theme never plays. A zone chime
queued for a bar line after the cut is let go: it was built in the climb's key.

**The key it takes** (`keyFromCut`): its tonic on the tonic that was sounding, so the
lament is the parallel minor of whatever the climb was in -- E minor out of the march, D
minor out of the gallop, E minor out of the Lydian heavens, each plus its rung of the
ladder -- folded to within -6..+5 semitones of E so it keeps its register. The key is held
(`pinned`) until the music stops, so the impact's `resetKeys()`, which clears the live key
for the next run, cannot move it, and neither can the landing or a skipped fall. While it
is held, `transpose` IS the lament's key: it was kept beside `transpose` at first, which
went on reporting the live key -- E after the impact while the lament played in, say, C
minor -- and every effect is tuned by it, so the splat's dazed notes, the scoreboard's toll
and the menu ticks over it would have clashed. The wail, too, starts from the key the
lament is going to.

**Letting go.** `stopMusic()` releases the playing buses, a handover's and the fall's cut
under the stab, over `STOP_RELEASE` (0.12 s); the next track starts 0.08 s on. It used to
let them go at full level so that queued notes rang out, harmless under the old
soundtrack's short notes; a lament's choir holds a chord for a bar, and Space on the
scoreboard left it ringing up to 3.2 s into the new run's march, in the last climb's key,
2 to 6 dB under the march.

**Its level**, measured through the engine itself at the scoreboard's intensity 0: -37.9
LUFS over the loop, 2.6 LU under the climb themes' mean (-35.3 at a steady 0.2) and 2.1
under the quietest (the gallop, -35.8), peaking at -23.8 dBFS against their -22.9. Voice
by voice over the loop: the organ -41.0, the pedal -43.9, the music box -46.4 and the
choir, which sounds in half of it, -46.3 (-48.6 while it was on the pad envelope). The
fall is -40.4 to -42.6 LUFS under the wail, and the stab's first 0.4 s -35.4 to -35.9. The mirror renderer (`music-render.mjs`) agrees with
the engine to 0.1 LU on the loop.

## The effects

Every effect is DATA in `src/render/sfx.js`: `make(params, key)` turns what happened (how
hard he jumped, how far he fell, how long the chain was) and the key the music is in into
a list of voices -- square, triangle and sawtooth oscillators with the music's hard attack
and exponential tail, a sample-and-hold noise for the NES noise channel, an optional filter
and pitch LFO -- with pitch and gain as WebAudio automation. `Audio.sfx()` plays the list on
the audio clock, and `tools/sfx-render.mjs` renders the same list offline, so a preview and
the game cannot disagree about what a sound is.

It replaced thirteen functions that built their nodes by hand and sequenced their notes
with `setTimeout`: off the audio clock, so an arpeggio smeared whenever a frame ran long
and kept firing through a pause, and impossible to render, so no effect had ever been
measured against the music. Measured once they could be, the zone chime was an A major
arpeggio over music in E minor, 4.4 LU louder than the music; the wail was 6.2 over it; a
landing 17 under; and a landing, a hop of the chain, the chain breaking, CLIMB!, the catch,
the scoreboard and a companion speaking made no sound at all.

**In key.** Anything with a pitch that means something -- the chime, the fanfares, the
chain's ladder, the payout, the voices, the menus -- is written in scale steps of the key
the music is SOUNDING in when the effect sounds (`Audio.keyNow(t)`): the live key; a change
waiting for its bar line from that bar line on; during a handover, the outgoing theme's
until its cut and the new theme's from a microsecond before it (the cut can round to just
under itself); the lament's held key; and with no music, the key of the track the game
wants. `TRACK_KEYS` in `sfx.js` files each track's home tonic and mode, and test-sfx checks
it against the notes in `tracks.js`, so a recomposed theme in a new key fails a test instead
of putting every chime out of tune with it. Only the percussive sounds -- a landing, a thud,
the splat, the catch -- are unpitched.

### What sounds when

`src/render/gamesounds.js` (`wireGameAudio`) wires a Game from outside: it wraps
`handleEvents`, reading the player's events before they are drained, and `step`, diffing
what changed, and installs the hooks the game calls (`onAnnounce`, `onFallStart`,
`onImpact` ...). The physics path still has no audio in it. It was a block in `main.js`
that compared five run counters before and after `handleEvents`, and everything that
changed outside it fell through: a chain banked by standing still -- `Game.step` closes it
before `handleEvents` runs, so the commonest way a chain ends was silent -- CLIMB! and the
scoreboard, along with everything that is not a counter (how far a landing fell, how hard
the combo drove a bounce). It is a module so a test can wire a real Game to it; the menus'
sounds stay in `main.js`, which owns the keys.

| method (effect) | plays when |
|---|---|
| `sfxJump(power)` | a jump off the ground: a square sweeping up from a chord tone set by how fast he was running, the tonic for a walk, the fifth for a sprint |
| `sfxDouble`, `sfxTriple` | the air jumps, TWICE and THRICE! |
| `sfxLand(weight)` | every landing, lower, longer and louder the further he dropped from the top of the arc (`landWeight`: 0 at 15 world units, 1 from 300) |
| `sfxWall(speed, boost)` | a wall bounce: the clang, and a ring in key that grows with the combo's drive, an octave sparkle on it from 0.85 (the white BOUNCE!) |
| `sfxWallKick` | a kick off a wall on the ground: a scuff |
| `sfxHop(progress, first, step)` | a landing that carries the chain on: a note up a ladder from the tonic to the octave across the `MULT_STEP` (50) floors to the next multiplier step; the chain opening picks up from the fifth below, and the hop that crosses a step is marked at the top of the ladder. Not on a landing that fired a callout |
| `sfxComboEnd(floors)` (`comboEnd`, then `payout`) | a chain banked, however it ended: the break, then coins run up the triad, three for the shortest chain to eight from 300 floors, a brass chord under a big one, and `PAYOUT_SWELL` (4 dB) louder at the top |
| `sfxMilestone(x)` (`milestone`) | a callout at the tower's heights (`onAnnounce`): below |
| `sfxTheme` (`zone`) | a zone arriving: a bell up the triad to the octave, on the bar line the zone lands on in the music |
| `sfxClimb` | CLIMB!: the rising floor is close, once a scare |
| `sfxJoin`, `sfxSlip` | a companion joining (the call's head, in the music's key) and losing their grip (a slide whistle down two octaves) |
| `sfxSpeak(voice, syllables)` | a companion's line becoming visible: clipped babble in their own register (`COMPANION_KEY`, taken as scale steps) |
| `sfxCatch`, `sfxScream` | the fire catching him, with `lament()`: a roar, and the wail, a sawtooth falling three octaves from the octave of the tonic the climb was sounding -- the lament's tonic -- held until the landing or a skip |
| `sfxFallWall` | the falling body glancing off a wall |
| `sfxImpact(splat, severity)` | the landing at the bottom: a splat bigger, lower and longer the further he fell, or a thud and three woozy notes for the dazed landing; the lament's stab is struck with it (`landLament`, just before) |
| `sfxGameOver({records, unlocked})` (`gameover`, `record`, `unlock`) | the scoreboard, once a run: a bell tolled twice, low; a new record's run 0.55 s after it, and an award's sparkle 0.55 s after that |
| `sfxMenu(kind)` | `main.js`: `move`, `select`, `back`, and `erase` for the awards wiped |
| `sfxPause`, `sfxResume` | the pause and the resume |

`sfxUnlock` is defined and nothing calls it; the award's sparkle plays through
`sfxGameOver`. `game.onComboStep` is set to do nothing on purpose: a multiplier step no
longer shouts a word, its sound is the hop ladder's mark, and the hook stays set so nothing
else fills it with a second sound for the same landing.

**The scoreboard's sounds belong to the scoreboard.** Leaving it -- for a new run or the
title -- lets go of the toll, the record, the award and the impact over 0.12 s, the ones
queued and not yet started included; a player who went straight into the next run used to
hear "a new record" over its first second. And they ring once a run: the stats and the
options open from the scoreboard and hand back to it, and the wiring took every return as
the scoreboard arriving, so one death tolled three times after a look at each.

### The milestone fanfares

The seven callouts, SWIFT to GLORY, mark the tower's heights: k/7 of the 2300-floor lap --
330, 660, 990, 1310, 1640, 1970 and GLORY on the lap's top floor -- once each per lap
(`src/game/milestones.js`). They used to fire at combo lengths of 50 to 350 floors. The
fanfare is picked by `milestoneIndex(x)`, 0 to 6, from `MILESTONES`, the table that fires
them, and the sample names come from the same table, so neither can drift from what the
game announces. A lap's prestige badge does not change the sound.

Each fanfare is THE CALL, starting a chord tone higher than the last -- tonic, third,
fifth, octave -- and from there growing instead of climbing, which keeps GLORY's top note
at the octave above E5, where a square is still a trumpet and not a whistle: a snare from
CHARGE, a harmony a third under from SOARING, a brass chord under the last note from
RAMPAGE, a drum roll into it from CRUSADE, thunder under THUNDER, and for GLORY a second run
up the triad and the whole chord held. Each is also `MILESTONE_SWELL` (1 dB) louder and its
last note rings `MILESTONE_RING` (0.08 s) longer than the one before: the orchestration
alone moved the loudness only two LU from SWIFT to GLORY, and with the call speeding up a
little each milestone the bigger fanfares came out shorter, 0.95 s for SWIFT to 0.89 for
RAMPAGE. A callout is the only thing that ducks the music, 5 dB held for 0.9 s.

### Mixed by measurement

Nobody here can listen, so each effect's `level` in `SFX` was set by measurement against
the climb themes. The measure is each variant's maximum MOMENTARY loudness (ITU BS.1770, a
400 ms window) in LU against the themes' integrated loudness -- a short sound intrudes less,
and the measure says so -- and test-sfx holds every variant inside its effect's `band`:
frequent sounds well under the music (a landing -20..-6, a hop -16..-8, a menu tick
-18..-10), the moves in the middle (a jump -10..-4, a bounce -11..-5), the moments near it
(the zone chime -6..0, the catch -6..0, the fanfares -7..+3, the impact -8..+2), and
nothing over +3 LU. Each effect alone, and a voice limit's worth of it at once, on the
music's loudest peak stays under the limiter's -6 dBFS, so the limiter never catches an
effect and pumps the music down with it. `render-sfx --calibrate` prints the level that
centres each effect's reference variant in its band.

What the numbers caught: the full-drive bounce's ring first grew the bounce by six LU to
-2.6, louder than the zone chime and three of the fanfares -- and past about 220 combo
floors every bounce is a full-drive bounce, fifty a minute, so the commonest sound of a
long chain outranked its rarest moments; it grows by three now. The payout at one level for
every chain size came in louder than the jump after every short hop; it swells with the
chain now. The dazed landing at the splat's gains measured 11 LU under the music, a thud
nobody would hear land; it is 6 dB up.

### Voices, variation and the pause

- **A voice limit** on every effect (`limit`): a new one steals the oldest, faded out over 15
  ms. The old code had none, and a burst of bounces or landings stacked a copy per event
  into a buzz several times as loud as one.
- **A shortest gap** (`gap`): an effect closer than that to the last is dropped.
- **Variation** (`vary`): the sounds a player hears hundreds of times a run differ a little
  in pitch, level and timing each time, and a noise voice starts at a random point in its
  loop. The tuned sounds never detune: out of tune is not variety. `Audio.rng` can be seeded.
- **The pause.** `pauseGame()` plays `sfxPause` and then `suspend()`, which pulls the music
  to silence over `PAUSE_FADE` (0.04 s), takes every effect still sounding with it except
  the jingle, and stops the audio clock `PAUSE_HOLD` (0.2 s) later, once the jingle has
  rung. Nothing plays while paused. `resumeCtx()` brings the music back over `RESUME_FADE`
  (0.08 s), and leaving a paused run for the menu lets go of the pause. `suspend()` used to
  stop the clock at once, so the jingle queued onto a stopped clock came out at the resume
  on top of the resume's own; and effects in flight were cut mid-waveform when the clock
  stopped -- a click -- and played on at the resume.

## Samples

There are no recorded samples, and every effect is synthesised. The PATH for them is
built -- `audio.loadSamples()` and `audio.sample()`, an `assets/sfx/` folder, MIME types in
the dev server and the Electron shell, and the same 5.2 kHz low-pass on the effects' bus as
on the music, so a recording lands in the same tonal world -- and it is wired to ONE
effect, the milestone callout.

Only the names in `SAMPLE_NAMES` in `src/render/audio.js` are ever fetched: the catch-all
`milestone` and one per callout, `milestone330` to `milestone2300`, from `MILESTONES`.
`main.js` imports that list and loads it once, on the first key or click (`ensureAudio`),
trying `.ogg` and then `.wav`, not awaited, so a missing folder never delays the first
sound. A callout asks for its own file first and then the catch-all (`SFX.milestone.samples`);
a sample it finds counts against the effect's voice limit and ducks the music as the
fanfare does. It used to play outside the limit, so a second callout talked over the first
and nothing, a pause or a steal, could let go of it. For another effect a sample is two
small code changes: its names in `SAMPLE_NAMES`, and a `samples` function on its entry in
`SFX`, as the milestone's has.

`assets/sfx/` is gitignored except its README: game audio from a commercial title is that
publisher's copyright, and this folder is inside a git repo. The desktop build packs every
`.ogg` and `.wav` in it; the web package (`tools/build-web.mjs`) only files under a name the
game asks for, because the folder holds whatever was dropped there. **[assets/sfx/README.md](../assets/sfx/README.md)**
has the processing chain that makes a recording sit in the music's 8-bit world, and the
names above (it gave the combo-floor names, `milestone50` to `milestone350`, until
2026-09-28).

## Listening without ears: the previews

Nothing here plays a sound; each tool writes WAV files to listen to later, and prints what
it measured.

- **`node tools/render-music.mjs --out=<folder>`** writes `stage1-below.wav`,
  `stage2-above.wav` and `stage3-heavens.wav` (a loop at a steady climb's intensity 0.2, a
  second with the run going hard at 0.6, and the tail); `menu.wav` (the menu as the title
  plays it, at intensity 0, one loop and four bars of the next); and
  `handover-below-to-above.wav` and `handover-above-to-heavens.wav` (the outgoing theme at the
  rung it reaches by the end of its stage, +7 and +3, the zone arriving at 18 s with its real
  chime, the handover and the new theme). The themes and the menu share one gain, the
  loudest theme peaking at -1 dBFS, so they compare at their in-game levels. `--only=` renders
  a subset; the menu is always measured at the gain a full run gives the themes, because at
  its own gain it read -16.8 LUFS for a menu that is -14.2, a shortfall that is not there.
- **`node tools/render-music.mjs --out=<folder> --gameover`** writes `lament-twice.wav` (the
  lament from its fall, landed 2.2 s in, round the loop twice), eight `death-*.wav` (each
  stage cut at two rungs of the ladder, a fall during a stage handover and a fall skipped
  with Space, each with the stab landed as the game lands it, and the impact, the stab and
  the scoreboard's times printed) and `climb-again.wav` (the scoreboard, then Space and the
  next run's march), all at the climb themes' gain. Music only: the catch, the wail and the
  splat are `render-sfx`'s.
- **`node tools/render-sfx.mjs --out=<folder>`** writes one file per variant a player can meet
  (`VARIANTS` in `tools/sfx-render.mjs`: the jump at three speeds, the landing at three
  heights, the seven fanfares ...), `tour.wav` (every one in the order a player meets them),
  `tour-over-music.wav` (the menu's sounds over the menu, the climb's over BELOW, the
  scoreboard's over the lament -- **the file to judge the balance by**) and a `README.md` of
  the tour's times, what each sound is for, its key and its loudness. It refuses to write if a
  variant is missing from the tour: two once were. `--calibrate` prints each effect's level
  for its band.

Under them:

- `tools/music-render.mjs` renders the music by MIRRORING the scheduler, with the voices,
  envelopes, tempo and choir curves (`tempoStep`), handover constants and bus imported from
  `audio.js`, so a change there changes the previews.
- `tools/sfx-render.mjs` renders the effects from the same `SFX` data through the same bus,
  with nothing random unless asked, so a measurement is repeatable.
- `tools/engine-render.mjs` drives the REAL `audio.js` through a recording AudioContext and
  plays the recorded graph back into samples. A mirror cannot show what the engine decides at
  run time -- where a cut falls, what key a track takes from the music it cut into, what a
  later `resetKeys()` leaves alone -- so the lament's previews, test-lament and test-steady
  use this.
- `tools/dsp.mjs` holds what they share: band-limited (polyBLEP) oscillators, WebAudio's
  automation curves and BiquadFilters, BS.1770 loudness and a WAV writer. It came out of
  `render-music.mjs` when the effects needed the same oscillators and meter, because two
  copies of a square would be two chances for the previews to disagree about what a square is.

## Tuning knobs

In `src/render/audio.js` unless named otherwise:

- `TEMPO_MIN` / `TEMPO_MAX` (0.92 and 1.30, a multiplier on each track's written bpm): the
  range the climb's tempo leans across with `game.intensity`. `TEMPO_MIN` is also where the
  menu and the lament rest.
- `TEMPO_EASE` (0.15 a second), `TEMPO_BAR_MAX` (0.02 of itself in one bar as heard):
  how slowly the climb's tempo follows the run. `TEMPO_SETTLE` (2.8 a second): how fast the
  menu and the lament come to rest.
- `CHOIR_MIN` / `CHOIR_MAX` (0.06 and 0.30, gain on the choir bus) and `CHOIR_EASE` (2.2 a
  second): how far the choir swells between a crawl and full tilt.
- `KEY_BANDS` (semitones over the theme's home key, one rung per zone), `KEY_MIN_BARS` (8
  bars between two changes of key), `THEME_MIN_LOOPS` (1 loop before a handover).
- `HANDOVER_FADE` 1.5 s, `HANDOVER_SWELL` 1.2 s, `HANDOVER_FROM` 0.3, `HANDOVER_TAIL`
  0.08 s: the stage handover. `LAMENT_FADE` 0.15 s, `LAMENT_FROM` 0.4, `LAMENT_SWELL`
  0.25 s, `LAMENT_GRID` 4 sixteenths: the cut into the lament. `LANDING_LEAD` 0.03 s,
  `LANDING_CUT` 0.02 s: the lament's stab at the impact. `STOP_RELEASE` 0.12 s.
- `ORGAN_SPEAK` 0.03 s, `ORGAN_RING` 0.08 s: the `organ` envelope's speech and ring. A
  voice's `stops` (its ranks) are in its track's `mix`.
- `PAUSE_FADE` 0.04 s, `PAUSE_HOLD` 0.2 s, `RESUME_FADE` 0.08 s.
- `MUSIC_BUS`, `SFX_BUS`, `LIMITER`: the fixed levels and low-passes, shared with the
  offline renderers.
- In `src/render/sfx.js`: each effect's `level`, `band`, `limit`, `gap`, `vary` and `duck`;
  `MILESTONE_SWELL` (1 dB), `MILESTONE_RING` (0.08 s), `PAYOUT_SWELL` (4 dB). Move a level
  with `render-sfx --calibrate` and test-sfx, not by ear.
- A track's voice levels are its `mix` in `tools/compose-music.mjs`, set by measurement.
